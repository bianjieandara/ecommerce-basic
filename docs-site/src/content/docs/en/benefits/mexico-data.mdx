| **86.1%** of the population used the internet in 2025 | People aged 6 and over: 104.9 million. _INEGI, ENDUTIH 2025_                              |
| **88.9%** urban and **75.2%** rural                   | The gap is still there, but the countryside is past three quarters. _INEGI, ENDUTIH 2025_ |
| **78.3%** of households have internet                 | Twice as many as a decade ago (39.1%). _INEGI, ENDUTIH 2025_                              |
| **84.6%** used a mobile phone; **38.1%** a computer   | The phone is the device nearly everyone carries. _INEGI, ENDUTIH 2025_                    |
| **44.7%** of households have a computer               | 17.7 million households. _INEGI, ENDUTIH 2025_                                            |
| **99 million** social media identities                | 74.9% of the population; not the same as unique people. _Digital 2026_                    |
| **91.4%** of internet users use WhatsApp              | Population aged 16 and over. _Digital 2026 (GWI data)_                                    |

:::note[Two internet figures that disagree, and why]
INEGI counts 104.9 million users, 86.1% of the population aged 6 and over, from a 2025 household survey. Digital 2026
reports 110 million and 83.5%, but against the total population and with October 2025 data. They measure different bases, so
they do not contradict each other; this guide uses INEGI's when talking about people, because it is Mexico's official
statistic.
:::

## How to read these figures

There are four kinds of source on this page, and they are not worth the same:

1. **Official statistics (INEGI).** Surveys and censuses by the country's statistics institute. The most solid, but slow to
   arrive: the Censos Económicos 2024 describe 2023.
2. **Industry studies (AMVO).** The Mexican Online Sales Association surveys shoppers and estimates the size of the market.
   These are the sector's most used and most recent figures, but they come from an association of companies that sell
   online, and its full report is downloaded through a form, so some figures reach us through the press that covered them.
3. **Global panels (Digital 2026, by We Are Social and Meltwater).** They mix GWI surveys with the platforms' own
   advertising-reach estimates. Useful for comparing countries; not a census.
4. **Published rates.** What each company says it charges, on its own site.

What is deliberately **not** here: the figures that circulate in blogs with no study behind them ("64% of sales go through
WhatsApp", "40% of SMEs have no digital presence"). They could not be traced to a primary source, so they are not used to
convince you of anything.
